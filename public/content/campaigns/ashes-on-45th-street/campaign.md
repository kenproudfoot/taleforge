## Overview

Last night the Hartwell Building on 45th Street went up like a match: six floors of offices belonging to
Hartwell Holdings, the last respectable fortune in a city that has run out of them. One man died in the
fire, old Walt Kearney the night watchman. And Evelyn Hartwell, the twenty-four-year-old heiress who had
started showing up at the office at strange hours, was seen going in an hour before the blaze. Nobody has
seen her since.

By morning Fire Chief Tom Dolan has called it an electrical fault. The papers have printed Evelyn's photo
under the word TRAGEDY. Her uncle Gordon Hartwell, who runs the company now, is offering a reward for her
body. Everyone wants this story closed by lunchtime.

The truth: Evelyn had found the books that prove Hartwell Holdings has been buying up tenements, insuring
them to the rafters and paying Vincent Castellane's torch man to burn them down. Gordon had the building
torched to destroy her evidence. But Evelyn got out with the ledgers, and Castellane's men grabbed her at
the docks. She is alive, locked in a warehouse on Pier 9, and at midnight the warehouse burns too.

Play it as classic noir: rain, neon, smoke, short sentences, people who lie for good reasons and bad ones.
The party follows the trail from the ashes to a jazz club to the docks to a burning warehouse, and at the
end has to decide what justice costs in a city that does not want any.

## Opening

Seven in the morning and it is still raining. Steam rises off what is left of the Hartwell Building, a
black ribcage of girders behind a line of tired policemen. The air tastes of wet ash. Fire hoses lie in the
gutter like dead snakes, and a newsboy is already shouting Evelyn Hartwell's name.

Introduce each character with one detail, give each a reason to be standing at this rope line (their hooks
help), and let a uniformed patrolman wave them through, or try to stop them. Ask the first character what
they want to see first.

## Beats

### arrive-scene
The ruins. Firemen are rolling up hoses; Fire Chief Dolan is giving a statement to the press and keeps
glancing at his watch. Things to find with a check or a good question: the fire started in three places
at once, on the ground floor, the sixth floor (Evelyn's office) and the records room (INT or WIS, DC 13:
"this was set"); kerosene smell in the stairwell (WIS DC 10); the watchman's body was found by the loading
door, which was chained from outside (DEX or STR to get close, DC 13). A soot-blackened woman's glove,
monogrammed E.H., lies in the alley behind the building. Complete when the party has seen enough to know
this was arson.

### meet-chief
Fire Chief Tom Dolan. Fifty, red-faced, decorated, scared. He insists on the electrical fault. Pressing
him (CHA DC 16, or DC 13 if they show him evidence of the three fire points) makes him crack a little: he
was told to sign the report by "people you don't say no to." If the party is decent to him he slips them
the one thing he kept off the report: a Blue Lantern matchbook found in the stairwell, with a phone number
written inside. If they threaten him he clams up and calls the precinct, and the party gains Heat. Mark a
milestone when the party leaves with either the matchbook or Dolan's admission.

### first-clue
Whatever route the party takes, they put two things together: the fire was set by a professional (three
points, kerosene, a chained door: a "torch" job), and the trail leads to the Blue Lantern, a jazz club on
Crane Street owned by Vincent Castellane. Evelyn's glove smells of the Lantern's perfume, or the phone
number rings the club's back office, or Dolan names it. Optional scene: a visit to Gordon Hartwell, who
is warm, grieving, generous with money and asks to be told first if anything is found (he wants to know
what they know). Record the clue. Complete when the party heads for the Blue Lantern.

### blue-lantern
Night, and the Blue Lantern is half full: smoke, a five-piece band, bootleg gin in teacups. Ruby Lark is
singing. Castellane holds court at a corner booth with two of his men; he will talk if approached with
nerve, and he is charming, frank and dangerous. He did not order the fire for himself: "a client" did. He
will not name the client, but he will let slip, if bested in conversation (CHA DC 16) or impressed, that
"the Hartwell kid is worth more breathing." Ruby finds the party in the alley between sets: Evelyn was her
friend, Evelyn left an envelope with her (a page torn from the ledgers listing burned tenements and the
Pier 9 address), and the torch man is "Sparks" Malone, who is drinking at the bar right now. If the party
moves on Sparks, he bolts. Give the ledger page as an item. Record the clue. Mark a milestone.

### docks-chase
Sparks Malone runs: out the kitchen door, down Crane Street, onto the docks in the rain. Run this as a
fast chase, not a long fight: two or three rolls (DEX to keep up, STR or DEX to clear crates and a fence,
WIS to spot where he went to ground). Two of Castellane's dockhands may try to slow the party down. If
caught, Sparks talks fast to save himself: Gordon Hartwell paid for the Hartwell fire, Castellane's men
took the girl, she is in the Pier 9 warehouse, and "at midnight the whole thing goes up with her in it."
If he gets away, the party finds his kerosene cans and a work order for Pier 9 at midnight in his locker
instead. Record the encounter. Mark a milestone.

### warehouse
Pier 9, eleven at night. A brick warehouse with a rusted rolling door, guards on the loading dock and a
lit office upstairs. Sparks's kerosene is already laid. Let the party plan: sneak in (DEX DC 13 per
obstacle), talk their way past (CHA DC 16 with a good story), or go in loud. Evelyn is in the upstairs
office, tied to a chair, with the ledgers hidden under the floorboards; she is sharp, furious and hurt.
If the party wasted time, Gordon arrives early and the fire is lit while they are inside: everyone makes a
CON save (DC 13) or takes 1d6 from smoke, and they must get out fast. Mark a milestone when Evelyn is free.

### confrontation
Gordon Hartwell arrives at the warehouse with Castellane's top gun, Leo Brandt, to see the job done. Or,
if the party got Evelyn out cleanly, the showdown happens at Gordon's penthouse instead. Gordon tries money
first, then threats, then Brandt. Castellane may appear too: he will happily sell Gordon out if the party
offers him a better deal (the ledgers, or silence about his men). The choice of ending happens here: take
the ledgers and Gordon to the one honest assistant district attorney and the newspapers (justice), trade
Gordon to Castellane for Evelyn's safety and leave the mob stronger (deal-with-the-devil), or lose Evelyn
or the ledgers to the fire (too-late). Mark the final milestone, then call end_campaign.

## Endings

### justice
The ledgers reach the front page and an honest assistant DA. Gordon Hartwell is arrested on the steps of
his own bank, Chief Dolan testifies, and the families of the burned tenements get their day in court.
Castellane walks, as men like him do, but he remembers the party's names. Evelyn takes her father's seat
on the board and starts writing cheques to the people her uncle burned out. Outcome: victory.

### deal-with-the-devil
Gordon Hartwell disappears one rainy night and is never found. Evelyn is alive and the city is told it
was all an accident. The ledgers go into Vincent Castellane's safe, and with them half the City Council.
The party has a powerful friend now, which in this town is the same thing as having a powerful owner.
Outcome: bittersweet.

### too-late
Pier 9 burns at midnight, as planned. Whether Evelyn or only the ledgers were lost in it, the story the
papers print is Gordon's. He gives a moving eulogy. The rain washes the ash off 45th Street by Sunday, and
the city forgets, because forgetting is what it does best. Outcome: defeat.
