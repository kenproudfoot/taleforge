## Overview

Eighty years ago the cathedral city of Tolmere sank beneath Greywater Bay in a single night, and King Aldric
went down with it. Ever since, the sea has been eating the coast: a field one year, a street the next. The
fishing village of Brinewick is all that is left, and the water is at its doorsteps.

Tonight is the Dead Tide, the lowest ebb in a lifetime. For a few hours the old causeway stands clear and the
spires of Tolmere rise dripping out of the bay. Elder Maren, the village tide-warden, believes the curse is
bound to the crown the king was wearing when he drowned. Bring it back, she says, and the sea will rest.

She is half right. The party must cross the causeway, get past the sealed Gate of Bells, deal with a salvage
captain who wants the crown for her own desperate reasons, and face what is left of King Aldric on his throne.
The tone is eerie, wet and melancholy rather than gory: a ghost story with a hard choice at the end. The tide
is the clock. When it comes back, it comes back fast.

## Opening

Dusk in Brinewick. Rain has just stopped and every roof drips. The whole village stands on the shingle with
lanterns, staring at the bay, because the bay is leaving. The water draws back like a held breath, and out of
the mud rise black spires, a broken dome, and a causeway of pale stone running straight toward them. Then,
from under the mud, a bell rings. Nobody is ringing it.

Elder Maren Holt pushes through the crowd toward the party, a brass tide-clock in her hands. Introduce each
character with one detail, let Maren state the job plainly (reach the cathedral, find the king, bring back the
crown before the tide-clock runs out), and ask the first character what they do.

## Beats

### brinewick-at-dusk
The party meets Elder Maren on the shingle and learns the job. She gives them the tide-clock (the Tide
resource starts at 6) and offers Pip, the lamplighter's boy, as a guide who knows the first stretch of
causeway. Let the players ask questions. Maren answers honestly about the history and dodges one thing: what
her family had to do with the king. A WIS check (DC 13) notices she is hiding guilt, not malice. Out in the
bay, a second lantern is already moving along the causeway: someone else is ahead of them. Complete this beat
when the party sets out.

### the-causeway
A mile of slick stone between walls of standing fog, with wrecks and whale bones in the mud on either side.
Halfway across, drowned sailors in rotted oilskins (tide-wights, two for a small party and three for four
players) drag themselves out of the silt and attack. Clever parties can avoid the fight: the wights shy away
from bells and from bright fire, and they only want to stop the living from going further. Whatever happens,
the party finds fresh boot prints and a dropped grappling iron stamped with a V: Captain Varr's crew passed
this way an hour ago. Spend 1 Tide when the party reaches the city. Mark a milestone.

### gate-of-bells
The cathedral's sea-gate is a wall of green bronze sealed since the drowning. Three bells hang over it: one
gold, one iron, one cracked. The ghost of Brother Osric, the last bell-ringer, sits beneath them and cannot
rest. The gate opens if the bells are rung in the order of the tide (iron for the ebb, cracked for the slack
water, gold for the flood). Osric gives the riddle as a rhyme if treated kindly, or the answer can be found
in the carvings on the gate (INT, DC 13). A wrong order calls up a wave: everyone makes a DEX save (DC 13)
or takes 1d6 damage. The party may also climb the wall (DEX, DC 16) or find the hole Varr's crew blasted in a
side chapel (WIS, DC 13). Mark a milestone when they are inside.

### varrs-bargain
In the flooded nave, Captain Isolde Varr and her wreckers are winching open the stair to the throne crypt.
She has two wreckers with her (three if there are four players). Varr is not a villain: the bay took her
ship and her crew last winter, and a voice in the water promised to give them back in exchange for the crown.
She will bargain, lie, or fight, in that order. A persuasive party can win her over as an uneasy ally, trick
her, sneak past, or fight her crew. If the fight goes against her she yields rather than die. Spend 1 Tide.
Record the encounter result.

### the-reliquary
Beneath the nave lies the reliquary, waist-deep in black water, where the steward's ledger is chained to a
lectern. Reading it reveals the first secret: the king drowned his own city on purpose. Something long and
pale, the Vault Lurker, hunts in the water and strikes at whoever lingers. It can be fought, distracted with
light, or outrun. The ledger and a silver bell-clapper wrapped in oilcloth (the tongue of the Tide Bell) are
the prizes here. Give both as items and record the clue. Spend 1 Tide. Mark a milestone.

### throne-of-tides
The throne crypt is a cavern of barnacled pillars under the high altar, open to the sea through a rift in the
floor. King Aldric sits on a coral-crusted throne with the crown fused to his brow. He speaks slowly, in a
voice like water in a pipe, and he does not want to fight: he wants to be relieved. He explains the bargain
if asked. Then the Deep takes hold of him and he rises as the Drowned King. This is the climax: a fight, a
parley, or both. He can be beaten down (at 0 HP the Deep lets go of him), or reached with words by anyone who
knows the truth from the ledger (CHA or WIS, DC 16, with advantage if they name the bargain). Mark a
milestone when the king is free or defeated.

### the-turning-tide
The tide-clock runs out. The sea pours back in through the rift and the party has to make the final choice
with water rising around them: ring the Tide Bell with its silver tongue and give the crown back to the sea
(the-bell-rings), have someone take up the crown and the oath to hold the water back (a-new-warden), or run
with nothing as the city goes under (lost-to-the-deep). If Varr is present she makes her own grab for the
crown unless she was won over. Finish with a dash back along the causeway ahead of the wave: one group roll
or a short montage, not a long chase. Then call end_campaign.

## Endings

### the-bell-rings
The party hangs the silver tongue in the Tide Bell and rings it with the crown laid on the altar. The
bargain is paid back in full. King Aldric closes his eyes for the first time in eighty years and goes to
foam, the drowned bells answer one by one, and the sea settles back to its old shoreline. Brinewick is safe.
Tolmere sinks for good, but quietly, like something finally allowed to sleep. Outcome: victory.

### a-new-warden
Someone takes the crown, whether a party member, Elder Maren, or Captain Varr, and swears the king's oath in
his place. The sea stops at the shingle and comes no further. Brinewick is saved, but the bargain goes on
with a new name on it, and one person will never be able to leave the shore again. Outcome: bittersweet.

### lost-to-the-deep
The tide returns before the choice is made, or the crown is carried off, or the party flees. Tolmere goes
under with its king still bound, and by spring the sea is in Brinewick's streets. The villagers load their
carts and go inland. The bells still ring on stormy nights. Outcome: defeat.
