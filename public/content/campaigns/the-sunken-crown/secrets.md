## Hidden truths

These are for the Dungeon Master only. Reveal each one only when its condition is met in play.

### the-bargain
Tolmere did not sink by accident and it was not a punishment. With plague in every street, King Aldric
went down to the crypt and bargained with the Deep: take the island and everyone on it, sick and well, and
spare the mainland. The Deep agreed and kept its word. Aldric has sat on his throne ever since as the living
seal on that bargain, drowned but unable to die.

### the-crown-holds-the-sea
The crown is the token of the bargain, and while Aldric wears it the sea is held to the terms: it may take
the coast slowly, a little each year, as interest. If the crown simply leaves Tolmere, the bargain is broken
in the Deep's favor, and the sea takes Brinewick within the season. Bringing the crown back to the village,
which is exactly what Maren asked for, is the worst thing the party could do. Somebody must wear it, or the
bargain must be properly ended.

### the-bell-undoes-it
The Tide Bell of Tolmere was cast to ring oaths in and out. Rung once with its silver tongue while the crown
lies on the high altar, it declares the bargain paid in full: eighty years of a king's vigil is more than the
debt was worth. The king is released, the sea returns to its old shore and stays there. The king's steward
took the tongue out of the bell and hid it in the reliquary, because he was afraid of what else might end if
the bargain did.

### marens-debt
The steward was Corwin Holt, Maren's great-grandfather. The Holts have kept the tide-watch out of guilt, and
each warden has passed down the truth along with the tide-clock. Maren knows the crown must not simply be
taken. She told the party a half-truth because she was afraid they would refuse to go, and because she has
quietly decided that if someone has to wear the crown next, it will be her.

### varrs-voice
The voice that promised Varr her crew was the Deep itself. If she brings it the crown, it will indeed give
her crew back: nineteen tide-wights, hers to command, forever. It never said alive. Captain Varr's old
bosun is among the wights on the causeway, and an observant character may notice the Kittiwake's name on a
wight's cap badge.
