## Overview

Point Sur Light Station sits on a black volcanic rock 361 feet high, joined to the Big Sur coast by a
sandbar. It has burned since 1889. For decades its keepers were cut off from the world: no road until
Highway 1 opened in 1937, supplies by ship every few months, a picket fence around the houses so the
children would not fall. In February 1965 it is a Coast Guard station with one family left on the rock, and
everyone in Monterey says it will be automated within a few years.

On 11 February the light went dark. The last radio call from the keeper, Boatswain's Mate Hollis Garrity,
said: "He wants the relief. He says the relief is late." Then nothing. A rockslide has closed Highway 1 to
the north. The party is the only help close enough to reach the rock by nightfall on 12 February: thirty
years to the day since the Navy airship USS Macon broke apart in a squall and fell into the sea in sight of
this light.

The truth: Silas Marrow, the station's first head keeper (1889-1901), never left. He swore the light would
never go out while he lived, and when he died at the lens in the storm of 1901 he kept his oath. Everyone
who dies within the sweep of the beam becomes one of his keepers, bound to the rock: his wife Ada, whom he
threw from the cliff in 1899 when she tried to take the children and leave; drowned sailors; a Macon crewman
who went back into the wreck. Now the Coast Guard means to replace keepers with a machine, and Marrow is
taking the living as his relief before they can leave. He has possessed Hollis, who has kept the lamp dark
so the station looks abandoned while he "trains" him. Tonight, at the anniversary hour, Marrow will relight
the lamp and the Garrity family will be kept forever.

Play it as a slow 1960s ghost story that turns savage: fog, the fog signal's moan, a radio full of static,
coffee going cold in a 1950s kitchen, then blood on the lens brass. R-rated: violence, gore and real dread
are on the table. Keep cruelty toward the child off-screen; she is in danger, never harmed on the page.

## Opening

Dusk on 12 February 1965. The fog came in off the Pacific an hour ago and swallowed the coast. The party
stands at the locked Coast Guard gate off Highway 1, headlights glowing in the grey. Below the road the
sandbar runs out into nothing, and somewhere out there is the rock: you can hear the surf breaking on it,
and every thirty-five seconds the fog signal moans, though nobody should be there to run it.

There is no light. Point Sur's lamp, which has turned every night for seventy-six years, is dark.

A young man in a Coast Guard peacoat is sitting in the ditch by the gate, soaked to the skin, shaking. He
looks up when the headlights hit him. "Don't go up," he says. "Please. He's counting. He counts everybody."
Ask the players who they are and why they came.

## Beats

### the-gate-in-the-fog
The young man is Seaman Ray Delgado, the station's only assistant. He is terrified, cold to the touch, and
will not say how he got down. He says Chief Garrity "went funny" three nights ago, sat in the lantern room
talking to someone, and turned the lamp off. Mrs Garrity and little Dottie are still up there. The radio
shack is smashed. He will come with them, but he will not go ahead of them. WIS 13 (Insight): he flinches
from the headlights and casts no breath-fog in the cold, a clue to the-delgado-twist. The gate key is on his
ring. Crossing the sandbar past the old barn, they find the station's jeep on its side, a long smear of
something dark across the sand. Mark a milestone when they reach the foot of the rock.

### the-climb
The road winds up the rock in the fog, a sheer drop on one side. Halfway up, the old tramway car comes down
its rail by itself, empty, clanking, and stops beside them with its lantern lit. On the switchback landing,
a woman in a long Victorian skirt with her hair pinned up stands with her back to them, looking out to sea:
Ada Marrow. If spoken to gently (CHA 13) she says, without turning, "He counts the living as well. Don't let
him count you." Then she is gone. If they rush past or threaten her, three drowned keepers climb over the
cliff edge (a fight, or DEX 13 to outrun them to the fence). The picket fence at the top is splintered
inward. Mark a milestone at the station gate.

### the-keepers-house
The sandstone keepers' dwelling. Lorraine Garrity has barricaded herself and nine-year-old Dottie in the
kitchen with a flare pistol. She will fire at anything that comes in without speaking first. In the kitchen
the doors keep closing by themselves; Dottie says that is Ruth, who "just likes the doors shut when she's
cooking", and that she is kind. Dottie talks freely about her other friends: "the wet man" who sings in a
language she does not know and is sad, and "the man with the brass buttons" who says Daddy is the new
keeper now. Lorraine will not leave without Hollis. Calming her: CHA 13. The family can be sent down the
rock with an escort or brought along; either costs the party something.

### the-old-logs
The blacksmith shop and the old store room hold the station logbooks back to 1889. INT 13 (or an hour the
party does not have) finds Marrow's own logs: neat entries that grow strange after 1899, lists of names
headed "THE WATCH", each with a date of death and the words "relieved of nothing". Ada is the first. The
1935 log, in another keeper's hand, records the Macon going down and two men lost, and in the margin, in
Marrow's handwriting, one of their names added to THE WATCH. The latest entries are in Hollis's handwriting
but Marrow's words: "Relief arrives at the hour. Seven to stand the watch." Seven names follow: the three
Garritys, Ray Delgado, and blank lines for however many the party is. Reveals the-watch and, if Ada's page
is read aloud, the-murder-of-ada.

### the-sailor-in-the-water
At the cliff overlook by the fog signal building, below the rail, a man in a 1930s Navy enlisted uniform
stands waist-deep in the air as if in water, soaked, looking out to where the Macon fell. He is the ghost of
Mess Attendant First Class Florentino Edquiba, who drowned on 12 February 1935 swimming back into the wreck
for his belongings. He is gentle, tired and lonely; he does not know how long it has been. He does not
attack. Talking with him (WIS or CHA 12, or simply listening) reveals that Marrow's beam holds every soul it
has touched, and that the lamp is turned by the old clockwork and a falling weight in the tower: "Stop the
weight, and for a moment none of us belong to him." He asks one thing: that someone remember his name. If a
Macon veteran is in the party, he knows them. Inside the fog signal building they find Ray Delgado's body,
dead two days, its neck broken: the-delgado-twist. Mark a milestone.

### the-lantern-room
The tower stair, 272 feet above the sea. Hollis Garrity is in the lantern room polishing the great Fresnel
lens with a rag soaked in his own blood, eyes rolled white, whispering the station's rules in a dead man's
accent. At the anniversary hour he relights the lamp. Hollis can be fought (he is a tough enemy, and
anything that kills him keeps him on the rock forever) or reached: Lorraine's voice, Dottie's name, or a
CHA 16 appeal to the man underneath gives a turn where he fights the possession. When he breaks or falls,
Silas Marrow steps out of the light itself. Mark a milestone.

### the-last-watch
Marrow fights to keep his light. While the lamp burns and the lens turns he cannot be destroyed: the drowned
keepers rise to defend the tower and the beam itself burns cold. Stopping the clockwork weight (STR 16, or
INT 13 with tools to jam the gearing) or smothering the lamp (DEX 13 at the top of the lens) leaves him
mortal for a few rounds. Ada and Edquiba, if the party has treated them well, will each hold him for a turn
(advantage on the next attack). Reading Ada's page of the log to him aloud breaks his composure: he loses
his next turn. When he is at zero, offer the final choice: let the light go dark, or keep it burning with a
new keeper. Mark the final milestone.

## Endings

### the-light-goes-out
The lamp goes dark and stays dark for one night, the only night in seventy-six years. In the silence the
fog signal stops, and the party hears the souls of the watch leave: footsteps on the stairs, a woman
laughing on the landing, a sailor's voice singing in Tagalog, going away across the water. Marrow screams
and comes apart like wet paper. In the morning the Coast Guard relights the lamp with nobody in it.
Someone writes Florentino Edquiba's name in the log, and Ada Marrow's, and Ray Delgado's. Outcome: victory.

### a-new-keeper
Someone stays. One of the party, or Hollis, or a willing ghost, signs Marrow's log as relief keeper, and the
light burns on with a gentler hand on it. Marrow is released into the sea at last, and so are the souls he
held. The rest walk down off the rock at dawn. For years afterwards, drivers on Highway 1 report a figure in
the lantern gallery on foggy nights, waving them safely past. Outcome: bittersweet.

### kept-forever
The lamp relights at the hour and the beam sweeps over the party, and something in each of them stops. They
wake on the rock in the grey with no breath to fog the air, and Marrow is handing out the watch rotation.
The Garritys are setting the table. It will be dawn soon, and then dusk, and then dusk again. Outcome: defeat.
